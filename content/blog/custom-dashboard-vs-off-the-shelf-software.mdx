For **custom dashboard vs off the shelf software**, the verdict depends on one question: does a ready-made tool fit the way your business already works? If it does, choose the ready-made tool. It's cheaper to start and it's ready today. Build a custom dashboard when your process is specific to you, it runs every day, and every tool you've tried makes your team work around it. With me, a custom dashboard starts at $1,200 and takes 2–4 weeks.

## Custom dashboard vs off the shelf software: what are you comparing?

Most build vs buy software articles are written by analytics vendors, and they mean one thing by "dashboard": a screen of charts and numbers for managers. That's a fair use case, and for it, buying usually wins. Reporting tools that connect to your data and draw charts are mature and good value.

A small business usually means something else. A **custom admin dashboard** is the screen your team works in all day: new orders come in, someone updates the status, stock goes down, a customer's details are corrected. It isn't for looking at the business; it's for running it.

That difference changes the answer:

- **For charts and reports,** a ready-made reporting tool is usually enough, and cheaper.
- **For daily operations,** ready-made tools fit only when your process matches theirs. When it doesn't, staff copy data between the tool, a spreadsheet and WhatsApp, and that copying is what a custom admin dashboard removes.

The [guide to custom web apps for small business](/blog/custom-web-app-for-small-business) covers the bigger version, when the dashboard grows into a full app with its own users.

## When is off-the-shelf software enough?

More often than developers admit. I tell clients to buy when:

- **The process is standard.** Invoicing, payroll, email marketing and appointment booking work the same way in most businesses, and good tools already exist for them.
- **The team is small and stable.** One or two people using a tool at a per-user price is affordable, and nobody is working around it yet.
- **The process is still changing.** If you're still working out how you'll take orders, pay monthly for a tool while you learn. Build when the process has settled.
- **You need it this week.** A build takes weeks; a subscription takes an afternoon.

In those cases, the right SaaS vs custom software choice is SaaS. Spend the money on the parts of the business customers see.

## Is custom software worth it for a small business?

It's worth it when the cost of fitting into a tool is higher than the cost of a build. The signs I look for:

- **Data typed twice.** An order lives in the store, then in a spreadsheet, then in a chat to the person packing it.
- **Paying for seats.** You pay a monthly fee for people who only need one screen, or for a plan tier because you need a single feature in it.
- **The missing screen.** The one view your team needs every day, like today's orders by delivery city, doesn't exist in the tool.
- **Access you can't control.** Staff see more than they should, because the tool's roles are all or nothing.
- **Your process bent to fit.** You've changed how you work because the software couldn't follow.

When two or three of those are true every week, a custom dashboard usually pays for itself, because it replaces hours of copying with one place to work. The [real prices for a custom dashboard](/blog/how-much-does-a-custom-dashboard-cost) break down what pushes a build up or down.

There's also a middle path: keep the ready-made tool for what it does well and add a small custom screen for the part it doesn't. On the free call we look at where your data lives today and plan how to bring in only what that screen needs, so you don't have to move everything at once.

## What should you check before you buy a SaaS tool?

None of the comparison guides I read gives this list, and it's the one that saves you from switching twice. Before you subscribe, check:

- [ ] **Export.** Can you download all your data, in a format another system can read, at any time, without asking support?
- [ ] **Per-user pricing.** What will it cost when the team doubles? Price it at the size you expect next year, not today.
- [ ] **Roles.** Can you stop a packer seeing customer phone numbers, or a part-timer seeing revenue?
- [ ] **The one screen.** Does the view your team needs every day exist, or is it on the roadmap?
- [ ] **Connections.** Does it work with your store, payments and WhatsApp, or will someone copy data across by hand?
- [ ] **Cancelling.** What happens to your data on the day you stop paying?

If a tool passes all six, buy it. If it fails the first three, a custom build becomes a lot cheaper over time than it looks today.
