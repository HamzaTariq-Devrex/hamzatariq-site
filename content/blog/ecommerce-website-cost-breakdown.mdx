Here is my ecommerce website cost breakdown in one line: a custom store with an admin dashboard starts at **$1,000**, a full ecommerce app with customer accounts starts at **$2,000**, and three add-ons can move the price: extra pages at $50 each, content editing at $150 and a second language at $250. Everything else you'll pay goes to other services, not to the developer.

| Line in the quote | Price | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ecommerce store with admin dashboard | from $1,000 | Up to 10 pages, unlimited products, admin for products and orders, usually 2–3 weeks |
| Full ecommerce app | from $2,000 | Up to 15 pages, plus customer logins, accounts and order history |
| Extra page | $50 each | Any page beyond the package's limit |
| Content editing (CMS) | +$150 | Edit page text and images yourself |
| Second language | +$250 | The whole store in a second language |

Every project is quoted after a free call. The sections below show how those lines add up, what you'll pay to others, and how to compare my quote with anyone else's.

## Ecommerce website cost breakdown: three worked quotes

Most articles on how much does an ecommerce website cost give ranges from a few hundred to six figures. A worked quote is more useful. These three are examples built from my real price list, not past invoices, so you can see exactly how the lines add up.

**Quote A: a small store.** Home, shop, three collection pages, about, contact and a policies page: eight pages in total, well inside the store package's limit of 10. No add-ons. **Total: $1,000.** You pay $500 to start and $500 on delivery.

**Quote B: a store you edit yourself.** The same store plus a lookbook page and a size guide, so 11 pages, one over the limit. You want to change banner text and images without asking me, so it gets content editing. **Total: $1,000 + $50 + $150 = $1,200.**

**Quote C: a full ecommerce app in two languages.** Customers sign in, see their orders and save their details, so it's the full app package. It needs two pages beyond the 15 included, content editing and a second language. **Total: $2,000 + $100 + $150 + $250 = $2,500.**

The jump from A to C isn't the add-ons. It's customer accounts. The moment shoppers need to log in, the online store price moves to the app package, because sign-up, password reset, order history and keeping each customer's data private are real work.

## How much should a small online store cost?

For a custom-coded store with its own design, an admin dashboard and unlimited products, my starting point is $1,000. A builder subscription can cost less on day one, but then you pay every month and work inside its templates.

What a small store usually needs, and what it doesn't:

- **Needs:** a shop page with filters, product pages, cart and guest checkout, policy pages, and an admin dashboard where you add products, change prices and stock, and see orders. The full list of [ecommerce admin dashboard features](/blog/ecommerce-admin-dashboard-features), and which ones to add later, has its own checklist.
- **Can wait:** customer accounts, a second language and wish lists. Add them when customers ask for them, not because a competitor has them.

The cheapest way to keep a small store small is to decide the page list before the build starts. Write down every page you want, count them, and check the number against the package limit. If you land just over the limit, ask yourself whether two of them could be one page with sections; often they can, and the quote stays at the package price.

## What are the hidden costs of an online store?

They aren't hidden, they're just paid to someone other than the developer. I split them into three groups so nothing surprises you after launch.

**Paid to the developer, later:**

- New features, pages or design changes after launch, quoted when you ask.
- Ongoing maintenance after the 30 days of free bug fixes, if you want someone on call.

**Paid to other services:**

- **Hosting and the database.** The stores I build run on Vercel with a Supabase database. Both bill you directly; check [Vercel's pricing](https://vercel.com/pricing) and [Supabase's pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing) for current plans. A small store often starts on their entry plans and moves up with traffic.
- **The domain**, renewed yearly with your registrar.
- **Payment provider fees** on every order, charged by whichever card or wallet provider you choose.
- **Marketing**, if you run ads or pay for content.

**Your own time:**

- **Product photos and descriptions.** These decide how much the store sells, and they're the most common reason a launch slips.
- **Policies.** Delivery charges, returns and the countries you ship to have to be decided before checkout can be built.

## What does a cheaper quote usually leave out?

When a quote comes in well under these prices, compare what's in it, not just the total. These are the lines I most often see missing:

- **An admin dashboard.** Without one, every price change or new product goes through the developer, and each of those requests costs you later.
- **A page limit.** "A store" with no page count can mean five pages, and every extra one becomes a negotiation.
- **Revisions.** My quotes include 2 free revision rounds. A quote with none means every change of mind is billed.
- **A support period.** I fix bugs free for 30 days after launch. Without that line, the first bug after launch is a new invoice.
- **SEO setup.** My stores go live with SEO set up: real HTML for every product page, their own titles and a sitemap. A store Google can't read isn't cheaper in the end.
- **Clear payment terms.** Mine are 50% upfront and 50% on delivery. Be careful with any quote that wants the full amount before you've seen anything.

Also agree in writing who holds the code, hosting and accounts, and what a handover includes if you ever change developers. The right answer differs between developers; what matters is that it's written down before you start. ## How to compare two ecommerce quotes line by line

Put the quotes side by side and tick these off for each one. A quote that can't answer a line isn't cheaper, it's unfinished.

- [ ] How many pages are included, and what does each extra page cost?
- [ ] Is there an admin dashboard for products, prices, stock and orders?
- [ ] Are products unlimited, or priced per product?
- [ ] Are customer accounts included, or is it guest checkout only?
- [ ] Can you edit page text and images yourself, and is that extra?
- [ ] How many revision rounds are included?
- [ ] How long are bugs fixed for free after launch?
- [ ] What are the payment terms, and what's due before work starts?
- [ ] What's the delivery time, and what has to be ready from you?
- [ ] Which costs (hosting, domain, payment fees) are billed by others?

Run that list against two quotes and the real ecommerce development cost difference usually becomes clear in a few minutes.
