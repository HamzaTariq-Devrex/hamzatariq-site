## Search intent

Buy. A founder who has decided on Next.js and wants a number: what a developer will charge for their site or store, and whether to pay by the hour or a fixed price. They expect a price near the top, then what changes it.

## Top results

1. [Second Talent: Cost to Hire a Next.js Developer](https://www.secondtalent.com/cost-to-hire/nextjs-developer/): hourly rates by region and seniority, monthly cost at full time, project estimates in hour ranges; sells developers.
2. [Aalpha: Next.js Developer Hourly Rates](https://www.aalpha.net/articles/cost-to-hire-nextjs-developer): wide unsourced hourly and project ranges, remote vs in-house; sells development services.
3. [Digiqt: How to Hire Remote Next.js Developers](https://digiqt.com/blog/cost-to-hire-nextjs-developers): hiring process and staffing models, no prices at all.
4. [Upwork: Hourly vs. Fixed-Rate Projects](https://www.upwork.com/resources/fixed-price-vs-hourly-rate): how fixed-price and hourly contracts work, milestones and approval, who carries the risk of a wrong estimate; no figures.

## What they miss

- Every result with numbers is a staffing company selling hours, so prices are hourly rates by country and seniority, not what a website costs.
- None shows a fixed-price package with what it includes: pages, features, delivery time.
- None prices the add-ons that move a quote (extra pages, content editing, a second language).
- None separates the developer's price from running costs after launch (hosting, database, domain).
- None says how a change in scope is priced once a fixed price is agreed.
