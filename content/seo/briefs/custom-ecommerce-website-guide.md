## Search intent

Learn, early in the buying journey. A business owner selling through Instagram, WhatsApp or a template store wonders whether a custom-built store is worth it. They want to know what "custom" actually means, how it compares with Shopify, what it costs and how long it takes. They expect a plain definition first, then a cost and timeline answer they can plan around.

## Top results

1. [Shopify: Custom ecommerce development, benefits and steps](https://www.shopify.com/blog/custom-ecommerce-development): a general primer and a five-step process (requirements, design, functionality, testing, deploy). No prices, no examples, and written by a platform with an interest in you not going custom.
2. [Alokai: Custom ecommerce website design, best practices, benefits and costs](https://alokai.com/blog/custom-ecommerce-website-design-best-practices-benefits-and-costs): five benefits, key elements, common mistakes (including SEO basics) and a cost section that says only "tens to hundreds of thousands of dollars" before steering to its own product.
3. [RaftLabs: Shopify vs custom ecommerce](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/shopify-vs-custom-ecommerce): the most useful decision framework (stay on Shopify until its limits cost more than a build), with enterprise-scale estimates and one unnamed anecdote. Its figures disagree with each other between sections.

## What they miss

- Every result is written for enterprise budgets. None shows what a custom store costs a small or mid-sized brand, with a real price list and what each package includes.
- None shows a real store built custom, with screenshots, or a real client's words about the result.
- None describes the admin dashboard from the owner's side: what you actually do in it every day (add products, change prices, mark stock, process orders).
- Data and account ownership (domain, hosting, database, payment accounts) is not covered at all.
- Only one result says when a custom store is the wrong choice; the others argue for custom throughout.
