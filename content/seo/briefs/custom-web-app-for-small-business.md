## Search intent

Learn. A small business owner whose spreadsheets, chats and off-the-shelf tools no longer keep up. They want to know what a custom web app is, whether they need one yet, what the first version should include, what it costs and how long it takes. Many results for the bare keyword are marketplace gigs (Fiverr, Upwork, Contra), so the reader is also comparing who to build it with.

## Top results

1. [Arramton: Top 10 tips for developing a custom web app for small businesses](https://www.arramton.com/blogs/custom-web-app-for-small-businesses): generic list of tips (goals, budget, users, tech stack, UI, security, maintenance). Unsourced cost range in pounds and a 1–6 month timeline. No first-version scope, no "when not to build".
2. [WebAlive: 7 signs your business needs a custom web app](https://www.webalive.com.au/signs-business-needs-custom-web-app/): seven signs (spreadsheets breaking, workarounds, too many tools, scattered data). Market statistics mostly from vendors. No prices. A short "are we really ready?" section.
3. [Retool: spreadsheets vs custom apps](https://retool.com/blog/spreadsheets-vs-custom-apps): low-code vendor pitch; spreadsheet errors at large banks and agencies. Nothing on small-business scope or cost.
4. [RaftLabs: when does a business need custom software?](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/when-does-your-business-need-custom-software): the strongest; signals, a break-even calculation, a "when you don't need custom software" section. Its prices are illustrative ranges aimed at larger companies and unsourced.

## What they miss

- No real, fixed starting prices for a small business build; ranges span ten times or more and come with no scope.
- None says what the first version should include and what to leave for later, screen by screen.
- None compares custom code with ready-made tools and no-code builders side by side for a small business.
- None covers ownership and handover (who holds the code, hosting and database accounts) as something to agree in writing.
- Most never tell the reader when not to build.
