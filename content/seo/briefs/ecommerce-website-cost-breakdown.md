- Post: /blog/ecommerce-website-cost-breakdown

- Cluster: ecommerce (supporting, pillar: /blog/custom-ecommerce-website-guide)
- Intent: buy · Funnel: decision
- Secondary: how much does an ecommerce website cost, online store price, ecommerce development cost
- Researched: 2026-11-15 (web search, US results; the search tool doesn't show Google's "People also ask" box, so the questions below come from the top results' headings and FAQ sections and related searches)

## Search intent

Buy. A business owner with a quote in hand (or about to ask for one) wants to see what each part of an ecommerce website costs, what's missing from a cheap quote and what they'll keep paying after launch. They expect a line-item table first, then the extras.

## Top results

1. [Shopify: Ecommerce website cost](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ecommerce-website-cost): six cost groups (hosting, payments, design, custom development, add-ons, business costs), plan prices linked to its own pricing page, unsourced ranges for everything else. Written to steer readers to the platform's DIY route.
2. [Hostinger: Ecommerce website costs, breakdown and strategies](https://www.hostinger.com/uk/tutorials/ecommerce-website-cost/): essential and additional cost tables (domain, hosting, platform, design, payments, marketing, logistics, maintenance, contingency) and saving tips; promotes its own builder; figures unsourced and in places inconsistent.
3. [DesignRush: How much does an ecommerce website cost](https://www.designrush.com/agency/ecommerce/trends/cost-of-an-ecommerce-website): domain and hosting tables, four build models, agency vs freelance rates; agency-directory bias and a headline range that contradicts its own agency section.
4. [DigitalSuits: Ecommerce website development cost](https://digitalsuits.co/blog/ecommerce-website-development-cost/): three budget tiers, long lists of module, design and maintenance ranges, "launch budget vs ownership budget"; written by a platform agency selling retainers.

## What they miss

- None gives a fixed price from someone who builds custom stores, or a full worked quote that adds up line by line.
- None shows how to compare two developer quotes line by line (pages included, admin, revisions, support period, payment terms).
- None states page limits, revision rounds, the support period or payment terms, which are what make two quotes comparable.
- Their ranges run from hundreds to six figures, so the reader can't tell which line applies to a small store.
- Each is written to push a platform, a builder or an agency.

## Our angle

A line-by-line breakdown built only from my real prices (store with admin dashboard from $1,000, full ecommerce app from $2,000, extra pages $50 each, CMS +$150, second language +$250), three worked quotes that add up, the costs that are paid to others rather than the developer, and a checklist for comparing quotes.

## Unique additions

- Three worked example quotes that add up from real prices (store at $1,000; store with one extra page and CMS at $1,200; full app with two extra pages, CMS and a second language at $2,500).
- A split of costs into "paid to the developer", "paid to other services" and "your own time".
- A quote comparison checklist (`- [ ]`) covering pages included, admin, revisions, support period and payment terms.
- A real store example (Nagina Jewels, large catalogue) showing products don't count as pages.
