## Search intent

Compare. A business owner or founder who needs a Next.js website, store or app and is choosing between one freelance developer and an agency. They want a verdict first, then a side-by-side comparison, a sense of cost, and how to decide for their own project.

## Top results

1. [Digiqt: Next.js staffing agencies vs freelancers](https://digiqt.com/blog/nextjs-staffing-agencies-vs-freelancers): delivery risk, cost tradeoffs, SLAs, on-call rotations, compliance and IP assignment. Written for engineering managers by a staffing vendor; a contact CTA after every section.
2. [Paśko: Freelancer vs Agency, Who Should Build Your Website?](https://paskomichal.pl/en/blog/freelancer-vs-agency-website-development): short pros and cons of each, then a pitch for the author's own "agency quality at freelancer prices" offer. No data, no table.
3. [Clutch: When to Hire a Freelance Web Developer vs an Agency](https://clutch.co/resources/when-to-hire-a-freelance-web-developer-vs-agency): five pros and four cons for each side, then general factors. Balanced, but "agency" means a mid-size or large firm with project managers.
4. [Dribbble: Web Development Agency vs Freelancer vs In-House Team](https://dribbble.com/resources/tips/web-development-agency-vs-freelancer-vs-in-house-team): a TL;DR comparison table and price ranges per model, many of them not traced to a named source; ends in Dribbble's own hiring product.

## What they miss

- None covers the option most small businesses actually meet: a small, founder-led studio where the person who quotes the job also writes the code. They compare a lone freelancer with a large agency.
- None gives a decision by project type (landing page, store, dashboard, chatbot, SEO fix); they stay at "small project vs big project".
- None gives a real, fixed price for a defined Next.js project; the prices they show are wide market ranges, mostly unsourced.
- On code and accounts they either say nothing or talk about legal IP clauses; none gives a plain list of what to agree in writing (repository, hosting, domain, logins, what a handover includes).
- None explains what happens after launch (bug fixes, maintenance, who you message) in either model.
