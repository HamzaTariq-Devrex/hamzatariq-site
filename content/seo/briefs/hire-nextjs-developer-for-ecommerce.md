- Post: /blog/hire-nextjs-developer-for-ecommerce

- Researched: 2026-11-09 (web search, US results; the search tool doesn't show Google's "People also ask" box, so the questions below come from the top results' headings and related searches)

## Search intent

Hire. A store owner or founder has decided on a custom Next.js store, or is close to it, and wants to know how to tell a real ecommerce developer from a general web developer, what to ask and what it should cost. They expect a clear list of what to check, then prices.

## Top results

1. [Contra: Next.js freelancers for e-commerce](https://contra.com/hire/next.js-freelancers-for-e-commerce): a marketplace listing with generic FAQ answers about scope and contracts. Nothing specific to stores.
2. [Digiqt: Hiring Next.js Developers for eCommerce](https://digiqt.com/blog/hire-nextjs-ecommerce-developers): agency blog written for engineering leads: App Router, caching, Product JSON-LD, KPIs. No prices.
3. [Aalpha: Next.js eCommerce Development](https://www.aalpha.net/blog/nextjs-ecommerce-development/): the fullest result: checkout testing, caching, data ownership, partner vetting. Quotes five-figure agency budgets.
4. [iFlair: Hire Next.js Developers for JAMstack E-Commerce](https://www.iflair.com/?p=41398): agency pitch on JAMstack with generic hiring considerations. No costs or store-specific checks.

## What they miss

- Every result is written for someone with engineers on staff, or to sell an agency. None gives a store owner tests they can run themselves on a developer's live stores.
- None walks through the admin panel the owner will actually use each day: adding a product, changing a price, handling an order.
- Budgets are five-figure agency numbers or nothing; no result gives a real price for a small store with what's included.
- None says plainly when a custom store is the wrong choice.
