## Search intent

Hire. Someone needs a Next.js developer now and wants to know what to look for, what it costs and how to avoid a bad hire. The results page is mostly vendor landing pages (BairesDev, Toptal, Lemon, Fiverr) plus hiring guides from staffing firms. Readers expect skills, cost and freelancer-vs-agency first.

## Top results

1. [Adaface: How to hire a NextJS developer](https://www.adaface.com/blog/how-to-hire-nextjs-developers/): recruiter-style guide with skills, job description, job boards, its own paid skills tests and interview questions.
2. [SolGuruz: How to hire NextJS developers](https://solguruz.com/blog/how-to-hire-nextjs-developers): skills, testing candidates, common mistakes, rates by region, engagement models. The only one that tells the buyer to run Lighthouse on live URLs.

4. [Toptal: Hire Next.js developers](https://www.toptal.com/developers/next-js): marketplace page with a hiring guide, seniority levels, interview questions and payment safeguards.
5. [Aalpha: Next.js developer hourly rates](https://www.aalpha.net/articles/cost-to-hire-nextjs-developer): remote vs in-house cost argument; its own hourly ranges disagree between meta description and body.

## What they miss

- Almost all are written by firms selling developers or tests, for hiring managers with engineers on staff. Their tests (take-home builds, code review, technical interviews) need someone technical to judge them.
- None gives a non-technical buyer a full set of checks they can run alone on live work. SolGuruz stops at Lighthouse.
- None covers who owns the code, domain, hosting and accounts, beyond a passing mention of IP agreements.
- Post-launch terms (what is fixed free, for how long) are missing or generic.
- Cost is shown as hourly rates. Nobody gives a real fixed price list with delivery times and add-ons, which is how a small business actually buys.
- Nobody matches the checks to the project type (marketing site, store, dashboard, chatbot).
