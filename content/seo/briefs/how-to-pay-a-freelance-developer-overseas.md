# Brief: how to pay a freelance developer overseas

- Post: /blog/how-to-pay-a-freelance-developer-overseas

- Cluster: remote-developer (supporting, pillar: /blog/working-with-a-remote-web-developer)

- Secondary: pay international freelancer, milestone payments, freelancer payment methods
- Researched: 2026-11-25 (web search, US results; the search tool doesn't show Google's "People also ask" box, so the questions below come from the top results' headings, their FAQ sections and related searches such as "should I pay a developer upfront" and "milestone payments freelancer"; the Upwork guide in the results could not be opened)

## Search intent

Learn, close to a decision. A business owner who has picked a freelance web developer in another country and is about to send money for the first time. They want a safe method, a sensible split between upfront and later payments, and to know what can go wrong, in that order.

## Top results

1. [Remitly: How to Pay Freelancers Overseas](https://www.remitly.com/blog/finance/pay-freelancers-overseas/): marketplaces vs paying directly, bank details by country, compare exchange rates and fees; general, no checklist.
2. [Wise: How to Pay a Freelancer](https://wise.com/us/blog/how-to-pay-a-freelancer): contract, payment terms (upfront, on completion, milestones, hourly), payment methods, US tax forms; US-focused.
3. [Kiplinger: How to Pay Your International Freelancers](https://www.kiplinger.com/business/how-to-pay-your-international-freelancers): Wise, PayPal, wires and money orders with fee figures from 2022; no advice on deposits or milestones.
4. [Twine: When Should I Pay My Freelancer?](https://twine.net/blog/when-should-i-pay-my-freelancer): deposit, instalments, paying at the end, retainers; no methods or fees.
5. [Remitly Business: Agree on Payment Terms Before an Overseas Contractor Starts Work](https://www.remitly.com/blog/business/payment-terms-overseas-contractors/): six terms to agree, invoice contents, wire fee options (sender pays, shared, recipient pays), warning about "new bank details" emails.

## What they miss

- All are written by payment companies or marketplaces for businesses paying many contractors. None is written from the developer's side of a single fixed-price website project.
- None ties each payment to something you can check: what you should have in hand before you send it.
- None covers the running costs after launch (hosting, database, domain) that are paid to providers, not to the developer, and how to keep those apart from the project price.
- Almost none explains how revisions and post-launch fixes relate to the final payment.
