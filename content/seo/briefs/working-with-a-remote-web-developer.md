## Search intent

Learn. A business owner (often in the US, UK, Europe or the Middle East) is thinking about hiring a web developer in another country, usually to get good work at a lower price, and is nervous about it. They want to know whether it's safe, how payment works across borders, how time zones affect the project and what to agree in writing. They expect a reassuring but honest overview, then practical steps.

## Top results

1. [Software Planet Group: 15 tips for working with remote developers](https://softwareplanetgroup.co.uk/15-tips-for-working-with-remote-developers/): fifteen management tips (overlap hours, tools, goals, trust, NDAs) for a company managing a remote team. Written for 2022 and for team augmentation, with no payment, ownership or handover advice; ends in an outsourcing sales pitch.
2. [Oyster: Guide to working with freelancers based overseas](https://www.oysterhr.com/library/working-with-overseas-freelancers): compliance view: vetting, misclassification, contract clauses, time zones and paying across borders (bank transfer only). US-centric and a sales funnel for an employer-of-record platform.
3. [Codeable: What you need to know before starting your website development project](https://www.codeable.io/blog/what-you-need-development-project/): a WordPress-focused briefing checklist (budget, goals, deadlines, brief, logins and assets). No payments, contracts, ownership or time zones.

## What they miss

- None is written from the remote developer's side, so none tells the owner what a good remote developer will actually do: how payments, updates and handover work in a real project.
- None names concrete payment methods a developer abroad can receive, or how to split payments to limit risk.
- Ownership of the domain, hosting, database and code, and the handover at the end, are missing from all three.
- Warning signs of a bad or fake remote hire are not covered.
- Time zones are reduced to "make hours overlap", with nothing on how to run a project that doesn't need much overlap.

## Our angle

The view from the other side of the call: a developer based in Pakistan who works with clients abroad explains how he runs remote projects (payments by Payoneer or bank transfer, 50/50 terms, progress reviews, accounts in the client's name, handover), and what an owner should demand from any remote developer.

## Unique additions

- Real payment methods and terms from a working remote developer (Payoneer, bank transfer, 50% upfront and 50% on delivery, 2 revision rounds, 30 days of free fixes).
- An account-by-account ownership and handover list.
- A comparison table: remote freelancer vs offshore agency vs freelance platform.
- Warning signs specific to remote hires, and a checklist before the first payment.
- A real client review about how the project was run.
