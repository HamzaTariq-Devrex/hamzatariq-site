## Why your own restaurant website with online ordering matters

Marketplace apps are useful for being found, but every order through them costs you a commission, and the customer's details stay with the app. When a regular wants to order again, they open the app, not your restaurant.

Your own site changes that. Orders come to you, you decide the prices, and you can invite regulars back with your own offers.

## How I'd plan it

First I'd look at how orders work in your kitchen today: who sees a new order, how it gets to the chef, and how the guest knows it's ready. The ordering flow on the site and the [order dashboard](/services/dashboards) are built around that, not the other way round.

Then the menu. Guests decide quickly, so dishes need clear names, honest photos, prices and dietary labels, and the menu has to load fast on a phone with weak signal outside your door.

Finally, questions. Most calls during service are about the same few things: are you open, where do I park, is this dish gluten-free, can I book for tonight. An [AI assistant](/services/ai-chatbots) can answer those from your own information at any hour.

## Getting found on Google

Guests search "near me" and for dish names. A fast site with your menu as real text (not a PDF), your address and opening hours, and restaurant structured data helps Google show the right details.
